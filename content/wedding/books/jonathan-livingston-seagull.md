I read this in school, maybe in sixth grade, picking it off the shelf randomly during some reading time. I remember finding the premise sort of funny, but having that amusement give way to wonder at the sense of seriousness this book gave me about living one's own life. Jonathan isn't good at being a seagull, at least according to the first group of seagulls he lives with. But by giving himself fully to what he loves and believes in, he finds his way into a life that rewards his dedication. I feel like that idea, that giving myself fully to the things I love will take me into worlds that are built around that love, has stuck with me more than my sixth-grade teacher could have expected.

— Andrew
