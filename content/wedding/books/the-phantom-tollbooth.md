I think The Phantom Tollbooth is kind of in the same category as Roald Dahl books like Charlie and the Chocolate Factory, where a kid goes on an adventure and learns morals along the way. But The Phantom Tollbooth is the only book I can think of that so directly addresses the real moral struggle of my life, which is the struggle to let it all be as exciting and meaningful as it can be. Milo is a bored kid who's checked out of school with nothing to compel him, and he's dropped into an adventure where everything around him rams home, in as silly a way as can be, that the real mistake he's making is missing out. Missing out on how amazing mathematics is, missing out on a rainbow, missing out on living a life where what he does and what he pays attention to matters because he's living in an amazing world and every second is precious. I've read it throughout my life, first to myself and then to my son, and each time I come away going, "shit, I want to live *more*." More loudly, more courageously, more honestly, just more. I hope it makes you feel that way, too.

— Andrew
