I'm just so jealous of Ursula K. Le Guin. The most human characters? Sure, she's got them. The most intriguing world-building? Yep. The fiercest interest in the most important things about being human? Yeah, all of her books feel like they're pointed straight at the center of the soul. The Dispossessed, though, is the book that I think has done the most to fundamentally shift how I think about politics and society. The caring but honest way that she brings the reader through two very different societies just floored me, both with the contrast between them and the core similarities they share as groups of humans. I think about this book every time I vote, every time I interact with my neighborhood, and even every time I bump into the organizational flaws of my workplace. And Le Guin did all that while telling an exciting story with just the most compelling people, and with a dash of a love story that even in passing makes my heart ache.

— Andrew
