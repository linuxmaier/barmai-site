Looking at the overall list here, I'm struck by how many of these books came into my life through my Dad. Redwall, The Dispossessed, Shark Heart, The Tree of Knowledge... But this is the one that means the most to me personally, and it's because of the context. If I remember things rightly, Dad gifted this to Hanna shortly after meeting them. I'd introduced Hanna as non-binary but I didn't have a big conversation about what that meant with him before he gave us this book. And then Hanna read it and turned around and told me I had to check it out. If you're not familiar, it's a book about a diplomat who is trying to create contact with a planet whose people are human-like, but gender and sex work differently for them: instead of having one gender/sex, they spend most of their time agender, only really adopting one or the other role for the purpose of mating. They can be either or both throughout different parts of their lives, sometimes being a mother then maybe being a father for the next child.

Le Guin grounds you in the role of someone who considers himself to be a neutral diplomatic perspective only to gradually and confusedly discover how much the framing of gender was transforming and inflecting every bit of how he sees and treats those around him, expressing better to me than any other work of art I've encountered the experience I had of slowly coming to understand what role gender plays inside my own worldview, and what I can do to interact or change that. Every time I see or think about this book, I'm really moved by how seen I felt by my Dad when I was reading it.

— Andrew
